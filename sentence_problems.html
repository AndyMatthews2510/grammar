The Five Sentence Problems

These are the most common errors in written English. Learn to spot them — and fix them.

① Fragment
An incomplete sentence — missing a subject, a verb, or both. Often a dependent clause left on its own.
❌"Running through the park."(no subject)
❌"Because she was tired."(dependent clause alone)
✓"She was running through the park."
② Run-on Sentence
Two independent clauses joined with no punctuation or connector at all.
❌"I was tired I went to bed."
✓"I was tired, so I went to bed."
③ Comma Splice
Two independent clauses joined with only a comma — no coordinator or semicolon.
❌"I forgot my keys, I had to go back inside."
✓"I forgot my keys, so I had to go back inside."
④ Choppy Sentences
Too many very short sentences in a row. The writing feels jerky and disconnected.
❌"He woke up. He ate. He left."
✓"After waking up and eating, he left."
⑤ Stringy Sentence
Too many clauses chained together with "and" or "and so." The sentence never seems to end.
❌"She got up and she had breakfast and she went to school and she studied all day."
✓"After getting up and having breakfast, she went to school, where she studied all day."

Parallelism

When you list or compare ideas, every item must have the same grammatical shape.

Parallelism means that words, phrases, or clauses in a list or pair must use the same grammatical form. If you start with a verb ending in -ing, every item in that list should end in -ing. Mixing forms creates a jarring mismatch.
In lists
❌"She enjoys swimming, hiking, and to read."
✓"She enjoys swimming, hiking, and reading."
In pairs
❌"He is tall, athletic, and has intelligence."
✓"He is tall, athletic, and intelligent."
In comparisons
❌"She prefers cooking to eat out."
✓"She prefers cooking to eating out."
💡 Think of parallelism like a set of matching luggage — every piece should look like it belongs to the same set. One odd bag stands out immediately.
🔑 Quick check: Find the list or pair. Read just the items. Ask: are they all the same grammatical shape? If not, make them match.

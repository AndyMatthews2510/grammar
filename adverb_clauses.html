Adverb Clauses

Connecting ideas — and making your sentences do more work.

She studied hard because she wanted to pass the exam.

What Is an Adverb Clause?

No jargon — here's the simple version.

A group of words that gives extra information about when, where, why, how, or under what conditions something happens — and it always starts with a connector word.
💡 Think of it as a "why / when / how" add-on attached to your main sentence.
Without the clause
"She cried."
OK — but we don't know why.
With the clause
"She cried because she was happy."
✓ Now we know the reason!

The Four Types

Each type of connector word tells you what kind of information is being added.

⏰ Time
when · while · after · before · since · until · as soon as
"He called after he arrived."
❓ Reason
because · since · as
"I stayed because it was raining."
🔀 Condition
if · unless · even if · provided that
"We'll go if the weather is good."
⚡ Contrast
although · even though · whereas · while
"She smiled although she was tired."

Where Does the Clause Go?

The clause can come before OR after the main sentence — but the comma rule changes!

no comma
Pattern 1
Main Clause + Connector + Adverb Clause
She went home  after  the class ended.
comma ✓
Pattern 2
Connector + Adverb Clause , Main Clause
After the class ended,  she went home.
🔑 Key rule: When the adverb clause comes first, put a comma after it. When it comes last, no comma needed.

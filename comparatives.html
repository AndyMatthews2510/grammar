Comparatives &
Superlatives

How to compare two things — and how to rank one above all others.

Comparative: This road is longer than the other one.
Superlative: This is the longest road in the country.

Comparatives vs Superlatives

Two different jobs — but built from the same adjective.

A comparative compares two things: "This book is longer than that one."
A superlative ranks one thing above all others in a group: "This is the longest book I own."

Both are formed from the same adjective — the ending or structure changes depending on how many syllables the adjective has.
💡 Think of it as a race. A comparative tells you who came first out of two. A superlative tells you who came first out of everyone.

How to Form Them

The rule depends on how long the adjective is. Follow the pattern for each type.

Type of adjectiveComparativeSuperlative
One syllable
tall, fast, old, cheap		 taller, faster, older, cheaper
add -er		 the tallest, the fastest
add the -est
One syllable ending in -e
large, nice, wide		 larger, nicer, wider
add -r only		 the largest, the nicest
add the -st only
Short: consonant–vowel–consonant
big, hot, thin, sad		 bigger, hotter, thinner
double the final consonant, then -er		 the biggest, the hottest
double + the -est
Two syllables ending in -y
happy, funny, easy, busy		 happier, funnier, easier
change y → ier		 the happiest, the funniest
change y → iest
Two or more syllables (not -y)
beautiful, expensive, interesting		 more beautiful, more expensive
use more before the adjective		 the most beautiful, the most expensive
use the most before the adjective
Irregular
good, bad, far, little, much / many		 better, worse, farther/further, less, more the best, the worst, the farthest/furthest, the least, the most
⚠️ Never use both -er and more / -est and most
"She is more taller than me."  →  "She is taller than me." ✓
"It's the most cheapest option."  →  "It's the cheapest option." ✓

Using Comparatives

Comparatives have several useful patterns beyond the basic structure.

Basic comparison
comparative + than
"She is taller than her brother."
"This exam was harder than the last one."
"It is more expensive than I expected."
Making it stronger or weaker
much / far / a lot / slightly / a bit
"He is much taller than me." (a lot more)
"This is far more interesting." (much more)
"It's a bit cheaper online." (slightly less)
Getting more and more
comparative + and + comparative
"The weather is getting worse and worse."
"Traffic is becoming more and more congested."
"She was getting angrier and angrier."
One thing causes another to change
the + comparative … the + comparative
"The more you practise, the better you get."
"The harder he works, the more he earns."
"The sooner we leave, the earlier we arrive."
Showing two things are equal — as … as
as + adjective + as = the same degree:  "She is as tall as her mother."
not as + adjective + as = less than:  "This hotel is not as expensive as the other one."
Note: never use a comparative or superlative inside an as...as structure: "She is as taller as her mother." ✗

Using Superlatives

Superlatives always use the, and often say which group something is ranked within.

the + superlative + in
"She is the best student in the class."   "It's the tallest building in the world."
Use in for places or groups.
the + superlative + of
"He is the youngest of the three brothers."   "It was the worst of the options."
Use of for a set of items or a stated group.
one of the + superlative + plural noun
"Bangkok is one of the most exciting cities in Asia."
Note: plural noun after the superlative in this pattern.
superlative + I've ever …
"It was the best meal I've ever had."   "She is the most talented person I've ever met."
Present perfect is natural after superlatives to express personal experience.
⚠️ Don't forget the
"She is best student in the class."  →  "She is the best student in the class." ✓
"It was most interesting film I've seen."  →  "It was the most interesting film I've seen." ✓
And always use than after a comparative: "She is taller me."  →  "She is taller than me." ✓
🔑 Quick check: Comparing two things → comparative + than. Ranking one above all others → the + superlative + in / of. Equal → as + adjective + as.

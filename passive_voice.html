Grammar Made Simple

The Passive Voice

Shifting the focus from who does the action to what happens.

Active:The chef cooked the meal.
Passive:The meal was cooked by the chef.

Active vs Passive

Both sentences can describe the same event — the difference is where the focus goes.

Active Voice
"The scientist discovered a new species."
Focus on the doer (the scientist). Subject = the one doing the action.
Passive Voice
"A new species was discovered by the scientist."
Focus on the result (the species). Subject = the one receiving the action.
The formula:  be (in the correct tense) + past participle
The "by + agent" part is optional — leave it out when the doer is unknown or unimportant.
TenseFormulaExample
Present simpleis/are + ppThe office is cleaned every day.
Past simplewas/were + ppThe window was broken last night.
Present perfecthas/have been + ppThe contract has been signed.
Future simplewill be + ppThe results will be announced tomorrow.
Modalmodal + be + ppThe form must be completed in full.
Present continuousis/are being + ppThe road is being repaired.

When to Use the Passive

The passive isn't wrong — it's a stylistic choice. Use it when one of these applies.

❓
The doer is unknown or obvious
"My bike was stolen." (we don't know who)
"He was arrested." (clearly by police)
🎯
Focus on the result, not the doer
"The bridge was built in 1889." (the bridge matters, not who built it)
📄
Formal or impersonal writing
"It has been decided that..." / "Participants were asked to..."
🔑 Quick check: Ask "who does the action?" If the answer is unimportant, unknown, or obvious — passive. If it matters — active.

Grammar Made Simple

Reported Speech

Telling someone what another person said — in your own words.

Direct: "I'm tired," she said.
Reported: She said she was tired.

What Is Reported Speech?

When you tell someone what another person said, you have two choices.

Direct speech — the exact words
"I love this city," he said.
"We are leaving tomorrow," they told us.
"I can't find my keys," she said.
Reported speech — in your own words
He said he loved that city.
They told us they were leaving the next day.
She said she couldn't find her keys.
When you move from direct to reported speech, three things usually change: the verb tense moves back one step, pronouns change to match the new speaker, and time and place words may change. The word "that" is optional: "She said that she was tired" = "She said she was tired."
💡 Think of it like passing on a message. If your friend said "I am coming", you wouldn't text someone else "She am coming" — you'd naturally say "She said she was coming." The language shifts to fit your new perspective.

The Tense Moves Back One Step

Each tense in direct speech becomes the tense one step further into the past when reported. Think of it as sliding one position back.

"I was there."

present perfect		 → She said she had finished.

"I must go."

"She said she should try harder."
⚡ When the tense does NOT change
If something is still true now, you can leave the tense as it is:
"The Earth orbits the Sun," the teacher said. → "The teacher said the Earth orbits the Sun." ✓
If you are reporting something said very recently, and it is still the case:
"She says she is waiting outside." (she's still outside right now) ✓

Words That Change

As well as tense, pronouns and time and place words usually need to change too.

Pronouns

I→he / she
we→they
my→his / her
our→their
me→him / her
you→I / he / she / they

Time and place words

now→then
today→that day
yesterday→the day before
tomorrow→the next day
last week→the week before
next week→the following week
here→there
this→that
these→those

Say or Tell?

Both introduce reported speech, but they work differently — this is one of the most common mistakes.

say
No person after say. If you want to mention who was spoken to, add to.
"She said she was tired." ✓
"She said to me she was tired." ✓

❌ "She said me she was tired."
tell
Always has a person directly after it — no to needed.
"She told me she was tired." ✓
"He told us the meeting was cancelled." ✓

❌ "She told to me she was tired."
⚠️ Quick check
"She said me it was late."  →  "She told me it was late." ✓
"He told to her the news."  →  "He told her the news." ✓
Memory trick: tell takes a person like a direct object: "tell someone something". Say does not.

Reporting Questions

When you report a question, two things happen: the question word order disappears, and the tense moves back.

Yes/No questions → use "if" or "whether"
Questions that can be answered yes or no use if or whether in reported speech. The subject comes before the verb — normal statement order.
"Are you coming to the party?" she asked.
→ She asked if / whether I was coming to the party.

"Have you eaten?" he asked.
→ He asked if I had eaten.
Wh- questions → use the question word
Keep the question word (who, what, where, when, why, how), but switch back to normal statement order. No do / does / did.
"Where do you live?" she asked.
→ She asked where I lived.  (not: where did I live)

"What time does the train leave?" he asked.
→ He asked what time the train left.
🔑 The golden rule for reported questions: the word order goes back to normal — subject first, then verb, no question mark. "She asked where I lived" — not "where did I live".

Reporting Commands and Requests

Commands and requests become tell / ask + person + to + verb.

Positive command
"Sit down!" he said to her.
→ He told her to sit down.
Negative command
"Don't be late!" the teacher said.
→ The teacher told us not to be late.
Polite request
"Could you open the window?" she said.
→ She asked me to open the window.
Negative request
"Please don't tell anyone," he said.
→ He asked me not to tell anyone.
🔑 Commands become infinitives: tell/ask + person + (not) to + verb. No tense shift needed — the infinitive stays the same.

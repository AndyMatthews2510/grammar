Grammar Made Simple

Prepositions

The small words that show how everything relates — in time, place and more.

"She arrived at the airport on Tuesday morning after a long flight from Bangkok."

What Is a Preposition?

Small but essential — prepositions connect a noun or pronoun to the rest of the sentence.

A preposition is a word placed before a noun (or pronoun) to show its relationship to another word in the sentence. Prepositions can show time (when), place (where), direction (which way), manner (how) or cause (why).
💡 Think of prepositions as relationship words — they show how one thing connects to another. Remove them and sentences quickly become confusing: "She sat the cat the mat" tells us nothing useful.

Prepositions of Time

The three most important time prepositions — in, on and at — follow clear patterns.

in
Longer periods
Months · Years · Seasons
Parts of the day · Centuries / decades
in July · in 2024 · in the summer
in the morning / evening
in the 1990s · in the 21st century
on
Specific days & dates
Days of the week · Calendar dates
Named special days
on Monday · on Tuesdays
on 15 June · on my birthday
on Christmas Day · on New Year's Eve
at
Precise points in time
Clock times · Meal times · Night
Holiday periods (British English)
at 9am · at midnight · at noon
at breakfast · at night
at Christmas · at the weekend (BrE)
for
A duration of time — how long something lasts.
"She studied for three hours."   "I've lived here for ten years."
since
A starting point in the past — used with present perfect.
"He has worked here since 2018."   "I haven't eaten since breakfast."
during
Something that happens within a period (not how long).
"It rained during the night."   "She fell asleep during the lecture."
by
No later than a specific time — a deadline.
"Please submit the report by Friday."   "I'll be home by 6pm."
until
Up to a point in time — when something continues and then stops.
"She worked until midnight."   "Wait here until I get back."
⚠️ for vs since vs during
for + duration: "for two hours"  ·  since + starting point: "since 2pm"  ·  during + named period: "during the film"
❌ "I've been here during three hours."  ·  ✓ "I've been here for three hours."

Prepositions of Place

Again, in, on and at are the core — but each has a distinct logic.

in
Inside an enclosed space
Rooms · Buildings · Containers
Cities · Countries · Areas
in the box · in the room · in a car
in the kitchen · in the park
in Bangkok · in Thailand
on
On a surface or line
Flat surfaces · Transport (public)
Streets · Floors
on the table · on the floor · on the wall
on the bus · on a plane · on a ship
on Oxford Street · on the left
at
A specific point or location
Addresses · Events / venues
Precise spots
at the door · at the bus stop
at school · at work · at a party
at the top · at 5 Park Street
above/
below
Higher or lower than something, without touching.
"The painting is above the fireplace."   "The temperature fell below zero."
between/
among
Between = two things. Among = three or more / a group.
"She sat between Tom and Sarah."   "He was popular among his students."
next to /
beside
Immediately to the side of something.
"The café is next to the bank."   "Sit beside me."
in front of /
behind
Position relative to the front or back of something.
"The car parked in front of the house."   "The garden is behind the building."
⚠️ in / on / at — the transport trap
in a car / taxi / lift  ·  on a bus / train / plane / bike / ship  ·  at the station / airport (the location)
❌ "She is on the car."  ·  ✓ "She is in the car."   (private vehicles = in; public transport = on)

Prepositions of Movement & Direction

These show where something is going, not where it is.

to
Movement towards a destination. (Compare: at/in = already there)
"She walked to the station."   "We're flying to Japan next month."
into /
out of
Movement entering or leaving an enclosed space.
"He walked into the room."   "She climbed out of the car."
through
Movement passing from one side to the other of something.
"The train went through the tunnel."   "She walked through the forest."
across
Movement from one side to the other of a flat surface or open area.
"They swam across the river."   "She ran across the road."
along
Movement following the length of something.
"We walked along the beach."   "Drive along this road for two miles."
towards /
away from
Direction of movement — not necessarily reaching the destination.
"She moved towards the exit."   "He walked away from the crowd."
past
Movement going by something without stopping.
"Drive past the church and turn left."   "He ran past me without stopping."

Fixed Preposition Phrases

Many verbs and adjectives always take the same preposition. These must be learned as fixed combinations.

Verb + Preposition
wait for · listen to
depend on · rely on
apologise for · pay for
agree with · deal with
believe in · result in
belong to · refer to
consist of · dream of/about
Adjective + Preposition
good / bad at
afraid / proud / capable of
interested / involved in
responsible / famous for
similar to · different from
full / aware / fond of
keen / dependent on
🔑 Key principle: With fixed phrases, you simply have to learn the combination — there is no logical rule. When you learn a new verb or adjective, learn its preposition at the same time: not just interested but interested in.
